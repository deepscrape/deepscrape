10. Cookies

We set cookies that are strictly necessary to operate the Service: authentication cookies once you sign in, and a security cookie that protects forms against cross-site request forgery. These are required for the Service to work and are not used to track you.

Analytics cookies are set only with your consent, which we ask for in a banner on your first visit. If you accept, we set a cookie named gid for one year and store a record containing a one-way hash of your IP address, browser, operating system and device type. We use it to count unique visitors and to understand how the Service is used. If you decline, or do not answer, no analytics cookie is set and no such record is created. You can change your mind at any time below; a decline stops further analytics records from being created and clears the analytics cookies this browser already holds.

Why this list is short

A site like cloudflare.com publishes around a hundred and fifty cookies across those four categories, because it runs a marketing stack beside its product: OneTrust for consent, Adobe Experience Cloud and Amplitude for analytics, LogRocket for session replay, Bizible and Salesloft for lead tracking, and DoubleClick, Casale, Tremor, LiveRamp and the social networks for advertising. Every one of those is a separate processor, a separate retention rule, a separate disclosure and a separate thing to delete on request.

We hold none of them, and the reason is structural rather than cosmetic:

  • Measurement is built in, not bought. Page views and unique visitors are counted from our own event stream into first-party rollups, so there is no third-party analytics vendor to disclose and nothing to send outside the EU/EEA hosting region we already use.
  • No advertising stack. We do not run retargeting, cross-site profiling or ad exchanges, so there is no cookie whose purpose is to follow you to other websites. This is also why no cookie here falls into a “targeting” category.
  • Every cookie has an owner and an exit. The banner lists each one with its retention, the categories that own it can be switched off, and switching them off deletes the cookies rather than merely stopping new writes.

The trade-off is honest: we know less about how visitors browse the marketing pages than those stacks would tell us. We accept that to keep the cookie list to eight entries and every one of them first-party.
